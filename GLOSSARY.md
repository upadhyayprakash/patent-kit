# Glossary

The words you will meet while filing, in plain language. India-specific terms are marked (IN).

## Who is involved

| Who | Role |
|---|---|
| **Inventor** | The person (or people) who came up with the invention. Only humans can be inventors. |
| **Applicant** | The person or company that applies for, and will own, the patent. Often the inventor; can be a company the inventor assigns it to. |
| **Patent office** (IN) | The Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM), with branches in Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai and Kolkata. You file with the branch for where you live or work. |
| **Controller** (IN) | The officer who decides on applications; forms are addressed to "The Controller of Patents". |
| **Examiner** | Reads your application after you request examination, searches prior art and raises objections. |
| **Patent agent** | A registered professional who may file and argue for other people. You do not need one to file your own application. |
| **eSign provider** (IN) | A licensed company (for example eMudhra) that issues your electronic signature after Aadhaar or PAN verification. |

## Patent, or something else?

Different rights protect different things. Many products need more than one.

| Right | Protects | Example | India law |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patent** | How it **works**: a new technical function, mechanism or process | A magnetic rail that locks a slider and closes a circuit | Patents Act, 1970 |
| **Design registration** | How it **looks**: shape, pattern, colour, ornament of a product | The felt texture, the colour of the sliders, the overall shape | Designs Act, 2000 |
| **Trade mark** | The **name** and logo you sell under | "TactMat" | Trade Marks Act, 1999 |
| **Copyright** | Original **expression**: code as written, text, artwork | The companion app's source code | Copyright Act, 1957 |

India has no "utility model" (a cheaper, shorter patent some countries offer). This kit covers patents only.

## The basics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Patent** | A right, granted for up to 20 years from filing, to stop others making, using or selling your invention in that country. |
| **Invention** | A new product or process that solves a technical problem. Ideas alone, business methods and "computer programs per se" are not inventions (IN: section 3). |
| **Novelty** | Nothing public anywhere, before your filing date, describes the same thing. |
| **Inventive step** | It would not be obvious to a skilled person from what is already known. |
| **Industrial applicability** | It can be made or used in some industry. |
| **Prior art** | Everything public before your filing date: patents, papers, products, websites, videos, talks, your own posts. |
| **Public disclosure** | Making the invention available to anyone not bound to keep it secret. Before filing, this can destroy novelty. |
| **First to file** | When two people file for the same thing, the earlier filing date wins (India and most countries). |
| **Grace period** | A window in which your own disclosure does not count against you. India has almost none: do not rely on it. |

## The application

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Priority date** | The date your filing secures for what it describes. Everything later is judged against it. |
| **Provisional specification** | A first filing that describes the invention fully but needs no claims. It secures the priority date cheaply and gives 12 months to file the complete one. |
| **Complete specification** | The full application with claims, filed within 12 months of the provisional. |
| **Claims** | Numbered sentences that define exactly what the patent covers. The **independent** claim is the broadest; **dependent** claims add details and act as fallbacks. |
| **Description** | The text that explains the invention well enough for a skilled person to build it (**enablement**). |
| **Embodiment** | One concrete way of building the invention. Variations are other embodiments. |
| **Invention disclosure** | Your private write-up of the invention, from which the specification is drafted. |
| **Abstract** | A summary of at most 150 words (IN), used for publication and search. |
| **Drawings** | Figures in a strict black-and-white format with **reference numerals** (100, 110, ...) that the description refers to. |
| **Technical effect** | What the invention measurably improves (speed, energy, errors, bandwidth). Key for software inventions (IN: sections 3(k), 3(n)). |

## Forms (IN)

| Form | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 1** | Application for grant of patent (the cover form) | At filing |
| **Form 2** | The specification, provisional or complete | At filing; complete within 12 months |
| **Form 3** | Statement and undertaking about applications abroad | At filing or within 6 months |
| **Form 5** | Declaration of inventorship | With the complete specification |
| **Form 9** | Request for early publication | Optional, after the complete |
| **Form 18** | Request for examination | Within 31 months of filing |
| **Form 25** | Permission to file abroad first | Only if filing abroad within 6 weeks |
| **Form 28** | Small entity or startup status | Only for those entities |

## After filing

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Application number** | Your 12-digit number (IN), starting with the year. |
| **CBR** (IN) | Cash Book Receipt: the payment receipt with your application number and filing date. |
| **Patent pending** | Accurate from the filing date until grant or refusal. |
| **Publication** | The office publishes the application about 18 months after filing. |
| **FER** (IN) | First Examination Report: the examiner's objections, which you answer within 6 months (extendable by 3). |
| **Hearing** | A meeting (often by video) with the Controller if objections remain. |
| **Grant** | The patent is issued. Renewal fees keep it alive each year. |
| **PCT** | Patent Cooperation Treaty: one international application within 12 months that keeps the option to file in 150+ countries later. |
| **Section 39** (IN) | Residents of India must file in India first and wait 6 weeks before filing abroad, or get permission. |
